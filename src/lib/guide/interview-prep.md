# Interview prep

Most interview questions are not new. You will be asked to describe a time you
disagreed with someone, a project you're proud of, something that went wrong —
in this interview and in the next four. **[Interview Prep](/applications/interview)**
is where you write those answers once and keep them.

The material belongs to your **profile**, not to a single application. Write a
story for one interview and it's there for every interview after it.

## Two kinds of material

**Project stories** are structured answers to behavioural questions, in the
**STAR** shape:

- **Situation** — the context and background
- **Task** — your responsibility or goal
- **Action** — what you specifically did
- **Result** — the outcome, with numbers if you have them
- **Reflection** — what you learned

The reflection is the part people skip and interviewers remember. Each story
also gets a title and a category — Leadership, Problem Solving, Teamwork,
Technical Challenge, Conflict Resolution, Innovation, Learning from Failure,
Achievement — so you can find the right one under pressure.

**Cheat sheets** are free-form: the topics you want fresh in your mind, the
questions you mean to ask them, the numbers you keep forgetting.

## Linking a story to a project

A story can name the work it's about — a project from a role, or a side project.
Worth doing: it makes the subject a fact instead of something the AI has to
guess from your title, and the project's own page will list the stories written
about it.

## Working on it with AI

Both stories and cheat sheets have the same AI editor, and it works as a
conversation rather than a button that overwrites your text.

- Ask it to **draft** from what your profile already knows, or write the fields
  yourself and ask it to **improve** them.
- Ask for **advice** and you get recommendations to consider, not a rewrite.
- Ask for a **review** and it tells you what a reader would find thin.

Every turn is kept as a **version in the thread**, with the changes highlighted
word by word against the previous one. You choose which version is the story —
including going back to something you wrote three turns ago. Nothing is lost
because you tried a suggestion.

It works from your profile, so a thin profile makes for thin drafts. The story
is still yours to approve: the AI is good at structure and weak on what actually
happened in the room.

## After the interview

What was _said_ in a real interview belongs on the application itself, under its
**Activity** tab — notes, a transcript, the recruiter's email. Prep material is
what you bring in; activity is what came out. Keeping them apart is what lets a
story stay reusable while the record of one conversation stays with the job it
belongs to.
